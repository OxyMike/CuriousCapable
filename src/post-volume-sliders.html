The four volume sliders on your phone, finally explained

Ringer, media, alarms, calls: yes, they're separate, and yes, that's why your phone "didn't ring." Here's the map.

By Michael·Aug 5, 2026·3 min read

You turned the volume all the way up. You checked. And still, your daughter tells you she called three times last night and you never picked up. Meanwhile, the phone happily blasted a video of a cat playing piano at full volume in the doctor's waiting room. It's enough to make you suspect the phone has opinions.

It doesn't. It has separate volumes, and nobody told you. Your phone keeps different volume levels for different jobs, and the buttons on the side only change one of them at a time, depending on what the phone is doing at that moment. Once you see the map, the mystery clears right up.

The four volumes

Ringer and alerts. This is how loud your phone rings when someone calls and how loud it dings for texts. This is the one that matters for missed calls.

Media. This is how loud videos, music, podcasts, and games play. It's the one you change when something is already playing.

Call. This is how loud the other person's voice is during a phone call. You can only change it while you're on a call.

Alarms. This is how loud your morning alarm goes off. On an iPhone, alarms follow the ringer volume. On most Android phones, alarms have their own separate setting.

Here's the catch. When you press the volume buttons on the side of your phone, the phone guesses which volume you mean. If a video is playing, you're changing media. If you're on a call, you're changing call volume. So it's easy to turn the volume up while a video is playing, feel confident, and leave the ringer quietly turned down.

Fixing the ringer for good on an iPhone

Go to Settings, then Sounds & Haptics. Find the slider labeled Ringtone and Alert Volume and drag it up to a level you can hear across the house. Right below it, look for a switch called Change with Buttons. If you turn that off, the side buttons will never accidentally turn your ringer down again. They'll only control media and call volume. For many people, that one switch ends the missed-call problem.

On an Android phone, press either volume button and look for a small icon (often three dots or a gear) near the slider that appears. Tap it, and all the separate sliders appear at once. Set each one where you want it.

Three other reasons a phone "doesn't ring"

If the ringer is up and calls still slip by, check these. First, silent mode: many iPhones have a small switch on the left side, and if you can see orange, the phone is silenced (newer models use a button, and you'll see a bell with a line through it on screen). Second, Do Not Disturb or a Focus mode, which shows a small moon or other symbol at the top of the screen. Third, a setting called Silence Unknown Callers, which sends calls from numbers not in your contacts straight to voicemail. That's handy for scam calls, but it can silence the pharmacy, too.

Take two minutes today, set your ringer where you want it, and turn off Change with Buttons. Then ask someone to call you from the next room. When it rings loud and clear, you'll have solved one of the most common phone frustrations there is, and you'll know exactly why it happened.
