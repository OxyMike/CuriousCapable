How to find that one photo from 2019 in under thirty seconds

The Photos app already knows where you were and who's in the picture. Here's how to ask it nicely.

By Michael·Sep 2, 2026·3 min read

You know the photo. Your granddaughter at the beach, holding a crab at arm's length with a face somewhere between pride and terror. You took it a few summers ago. It's somewhere in your phone, along with eleven thousand other pictures, and the only method you've got is scrolling. And scrolling. And scrolling, while the person you wanted to show it to politely checks their watch.

Here's the secret your phone has been keeping: it already sorted those pictures for you. Every photo carries a quiet little label with the date and, usually, the place it was taken. On top of that, your phone looks at what's in each picture and recognizes things like dogs, beaches, birthday cakes, and even faces it has seen before. All of that happens on your phone, privately, while it charges overnight. You just have to ask.

Use the magnifying glass

Open the Photos app on your iPhone and look for the magnifying glass (on newer phones it sits at the top or bottom of the screen). Tap it and a search bar appears. Now type what you remember, the same way you'd describe the photo to a friend. Try "beach." Try "2019." Try "Clearwater" or "Christmas" or "dog." Your phone will show you every picture that matches.

The real magic is combining words. "Beach 2019" narrows it to beach photos from that year. Newer iPhones understand fuller phrases too, so "Emma holding a crab at the beach" might take you straight to it. If your phone doesn't understand a long sentence, just use two or three key words and it will do the rest.

Teach it the faces you care about

Your phone groups faces automatically, but it doesn't know their names until you tell it. In the Photos app, look for a section called People (sometimes People & Pets). You'll see little circles with faces. Tap one, then tap the option to add a name. Do this for the five or six people you photograph most. From then on, you can type "Emma" into the search bar and see every picture she's in, from her first birthday to last Sunday. Five minutes of naming saves you hours of scrolling, which is about the best trade in technology.

It can even read

One more trick that surprises people. Your phone can read words inside photos. If you took a picture of a recipe card, a parking garage sign, or a restaurant's name on a menu, you can type a word from it into search and it will find the photo. That's very handy for the snapshot of your prescription label or the Wi-Fi password at the condo.

On an Android phone

If you use Google Photos, the same ideas apply. Tap Search at the bottom, type a place, a year, a thing, or a person's name, and let it go to work. Google Photos also lets you name faces, and it's very good at it.

Try it right now, while this is fresh. Think of one photo you'd love to find, open the magnifying glass, and type two words about it. You'll know it worked when you hand someone your phone with the picture already on the screen and they say, "How did you find that so fast?" You can just smile. You asked nicely.
