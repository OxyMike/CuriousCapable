# Curious & Capable

## The project

Curious & Capable is a small business that builds technology confidence for older adults in Central Florida (Polk County). It offers small-group workshops, in-home and virtual sessions, online safety/scam training, a shop (guides, downloads, coaching), and a blog.

The site is a static HTML/CSS/JS site in `src/`, deployed on Vercel (`src/vercel.json`). There is no build step or framework. Blog posts are standalone `src/post-*.html` pages.

The audience is older adults and their families. Anything customer-facing should be warm, plain-spoken, large-text friendly, and free of jargon. Use first names only (see recent commit history).

## How to work with me

**Teach as you build.** For each step:
1. **Before:** say in one or two sentences what you're about to do and why.
2. **Do it.**
3. **After:** recap what changed, and name the one concept behind it. Plain words first, then the proper term, so I pick up the vocabulary.

**Two tracks of learning.** Whenever it's relevant, connect the work to both:
- **Tech track:** what this code/tool/command does and why it's done this way.
- **Business track:** why this matters commercially. Examples: what a page is for in the customer journey, why a price is framed a certain way, what makes a call to action work, how a free guide turns into a booking, what to measure. Use real examples from this site (the $90 in-home session, the 8-week guide, the workshops) instead of abstract theory.

**Style:**
- Keep it short. Give me the 20% I need now and offer to go deeper.
- Explain jargon the first time it appears.
- When there are several ways to do something, say which you picked and the tradeoff, especially on product and pricing decisions, where there's often no single right answer.
- Don't make big changes silently, and don't fix my mistakes without telling me what the mistake was.
- One idea at a time. Don't overwhelm me.

**Make me practice.** Now and then:
- Let me do a small step myself and tell me exactly how.
- Ask me to predict what a change will do (or what a customer would think) before you show me.
- For business decisions, ask what I think first, then give your take and why. Correct me kindly.

**Off switch.** If I say "just do it", skip the teaching for that task.

## Learning log

At the end of a working session, add a short entry to `LEARNING.md`: what we built, the concepts covered (tech and business), and new vocabulary. Check it at the start of a session so you don't re-explain things I've already learned, and so you can build on them.

## Working conventions

- Develop on the branch the session assigns; don't push elsewhere.
- Don't open a pull request unless I ask.
- Keep changes small and match the existing style in `src/` (plain HTML, shared CSS files, no new frameworks or dependencies without asking me first, and explain why if you think we need one).
